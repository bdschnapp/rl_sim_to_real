LiDAR Hitch Angle Estimation — Method Comparison

Method 1 — PCA Face Normal lidar_hitch_angle.py
Trailer front face (viewed from above): LiDAR ● ┌──────────┐ ────── normal ←── │ TRAILER │ └──────────┘ PCA → eigenvec of min eigenvalue trailer_dir = normal × Ẑ angle = atan2(det, dot) vs truck +X
  • How: PCA on ROI points → smallest eigenvector = face normal → cross with Ẑ → heading angle
  • Pro: Simple, fast, works well when only the front face is visible
  • Con: At ~20–40° both front + side faces are visible; PCA normal becomes ambiguous and noisy
  • Con: Side-face branch is commented out → breaks beyond ±40°
  • Con: Binary ROI shift (±0.2 m) does not track trailer at large angles
  • Con: No temporal filtering → raw output is noisy frame-to-frame
Method 2 — L-Shape Rectangle Fitting hitch_filter_vis.py
L-shape visibility (moderate hitch angle): LiDAR ● ┌── front face ──┐ │ │ ← side face Score(θ) = Σ min(dx, dy)² minimised → rectangle aligns with both faces at once
  • How: Search θ ∈ [0°,180°); fit bounding box; minimise Σ min(dx,dy)² — closeness criterion (Zhang 2017)
  • Pro: Handles front-only, L-shape, and side-only point patterns in one unified pass
  • Pro: Trig-based dynamic ROI tracking — shifts proportional to sin(θ)
  • Pro: Moving-average (10-sample) + Kalman filter (const. angular velocity) smooth the output
  • Pro: Kalman innovation gating (3σ) rejects outlier frames before they corrupt the estimate
  • Tune: ROI bounds via ROS2 params — no recompile needed
